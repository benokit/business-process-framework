# entity-amendments

Changes to an entity with a versioned body as entities of their own: an amendment is proposed, issued, activated, and may be annulled. Activating one amends the entity from a date; annulling it takes the version away. The mechanism of versions is the trait `body` of [entities](../entities/README.md#versioned-body); the generation of the amendment from its entity and its effects on it are those of [entity-generation](../entity-generation/README.md), of which this is the special case, and this package is the policy and the lifecycle around them.

- **`amendment-ledger`** (trait, key `amendment-ledger`; the kind `amendment-ledger-trait`): the ledger `{ last, open }` as its value, the number of the last amendment and the key of the open one (`null` if none), with two operations: `open({ source, amendment })` is a `conflict` while an amendment is open, and writes `last + 1` and the key; `close({ source, amendment, revision?, proposedAt? })` is a `conflict` if the amendment is not the open one, or, when `proposedAt` is given, if `revision` differs from it, and writes `open` back to `null`.
- **`versioned-entity-type`** (kind, extends `generating-entity-type`): an entity type whose body is versioned and that can be amended; it fixes the traits `amendment-ledger` and `generator` (the instance `amendment-generator` of `generator-trait`, which declares the pair `apply`/`revert`) with the policy of amendments, which is all the generator knows about them (`$func/amendment-effect-operations` is the policy `effects`):
  - **propose** (the operation `generate`, with `context: { effectiveFrom }`, checked by the schema `amendment-context`): `conflict` unless the state model lists `generate` for the state (and `invalid` for a declaration the type hasn't, or an `input` that doesn't fit); the effect `generate` runs the ledger's `open`, so a proposal while one is open is a `conflict`, and concurrent proposals can't both win; the amendment is named `<businessKey>-<number>` (`%03d`, the `open` of the ledger), so the numbers have no gaps. The revision the link records is the one the ledger write made.
  - **apply**: the items of `apply`, then the ledger's `close` with the revision check: `conflict` if the entity is no longer at the revision of the link, or the amendment is not the open one; all in one change. Without items it is `body.amend` of the amendment's body valid from the `effectiveFrom` of the link's `context` and the key of the amendment as `ref`, which is how a declaration with no effects (`{ type }`) amends by copying the last version; `$func/patched-body` makes a body from the last version and a patch.
  - **revert**: the items of `revert`, or `body.remove` of that date and `ref`; only the latest version can go, so annulments run in the reverse order of activations (`conflict` otherwise).
  - **close**: the ledger's `close` when the amendment is discarded without having been applied.
  - **`validate()`**: besides the checks of [entity-generation](../entity-generation/README.md), an `apply` that changes a trait its `revert` doesn't.
  The concrete type configures the body (`versioned.validFrom`) and its state model, and says in `operations` in which states an amendment may be proposed and applied (`generate`, `generator/apply`); `generator/revert` left out means any state.
- **`amendment-entity-type`** (kind, extends `generated-entity-type`): the trait `generated` with the bindings `apply` (`versionStatus` `pending` → `applied`), `revert` (`applied` → `discarded`) and `close` (`pending` → `discarded`), and the dimension `versionStatus` of its `state`: `pending` (the change is being made or reviewed, not in the entity's versions), `applied` (its version is in them) and `discarded` (it is not, or no longer is), a graph the kind declares in its model ([restricting the moves](../entity-states/README.md#restricting-the-moves-of-a-dimension)), so that `validate()` reports a process that moves it any other way or starts it elsewhere than `pending`. The **process** is the application's own, in other dimensions (an approval with `status`, say) and in transitions of its own names, each of which moves `versionStatus` where it should; the body schema is the concrete type's own (a copy of the entity's, a reason and typed changes, …). The operation `preview({ businessKey, effect })` (of `generated-entity-type`) returns the commands that the effect, `apply` say, would write, `[{ key, command, value }]`, and writes nothing; who may call it is the authorization of operations, per state. An amendment can't be created other than by `generate` (the guard of `generated`).

What the package leaves to the application: an HTTP route for `generate` (`entity-generation-gateway.generate`), who may do what ([authorization](../../services/authorization/README.md), per state with [state-permissions](../../services/state-permissions/README.md)), the amendment types and the shape of their data, and how the `input` of the request becomes an amendment and an amendment a version (the declarations). The demo's declarations turn a flat `changes` object into a patch ([amendment endpoint](../../../demo/contracts/amendment-endpoint.eson)).
